Why now

Last winter the shelves were empty by February.

412 households came through the door between November and March, nearly twice the winter before. Heating bills went up, the school uniform grant went down, and by the second week of February we were turning people away with a voucher for somewhere else.

This year we want to open every day until the clocks change. That means a full larder from the start, not a scramble in the new year.

      Who keeps the shelves full

      “Tuesday mornings we sort what came in. By lunchtime it looks like a shop.”

      Samira, runs the Tuesday sorting shift

      Samira started coming in for the toddler group and stayed for the tea. Three winters on, she trains every new volunteer in how the shelves are laid out, oldest dates at the front.

      “Forty plates on a Thursday. Nobody’s asked why they’re there.”

      Graham, cooks Thursday supper

      A retired school cook, Graham turns whatever's on the shelf into a hot meal for anyone who walks in. Pasta bake is the favourite. £12 feeds a family of four at his table.

      “Kwame brings the crate and stops for a brew. Some weeks he’s the only one I see.”

      Maureen, gets a Saturday delivery

      Maureen can't manage the hill since her hip operation, so the larder comes to her. Our drivers take forty crates up the valley every Saturday morning.

      92p

      92p of every £1 goes on the shelves.

      The rest keeps the van on the road and the lights on in the old Co-op. Our accounts are on the Charity Commission register, and the total at the top of this page is updated by hand every Monday.

      • Food on the shelves, 72%
      • Gas and electric top-ups, 20%
      • Van and rent, 8%
